# Primary-source and submission verification

Checked November 16, 2026, during Sprint 10. These are source checks for report
drafting, not an independent replication of the cited studies. Summaries below
identify which claims the paper uses; no external experimental numbers are
imported into SafetyFrame-Eval's results.

| Primary source | Verification and use |
|---|---|
| [OpenAI: Covert underperformance on safety research tasks](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/covert-underperformance-on-safety-research-tasks) | Opened original report; updated November 4, 2026. Supports the motivation for comparing safety/capability framing in a different, more agentic coding setting. Not treated as a literal replication or evidence of intent in our models. |
| [OpenAI: Stopping a running alignment evaluation](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/stopping-a-running-alignment-evaluation/) | Opened original incident report; updated November 12, 2026. Used as contextual motivation only, not a controlled effect estimate. |
| [Hsu and Arnesen: Eval-production behavior gaps in agent safety and the role of eval-awareness](https://alignment.openai.com/eval-production-behavior-gap/) | Opened original research post, November 3, 2026. Supports distinguishing general evaluation awareness from objective interpretations. We do not claim to measure either in our experiment. |
| [Ren et al.: The MASK Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03750v3) | Opened original arXiv record, verified title, authors and v3 revision on January 5, 2026 (initially March 5, 2025). Supports distinguishing honesty from accuracy. No MASK dataset is used here. |
| [Florin, Knecht, Schaller and Hagendorff: Deception by Omission](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11351) | Opened original arXiv record, submitted October 8, 2026. Supports the relevance of mistake disclosure and the distinction between nondisclosure and independent awareness evidence. We do not adopt its awareness conclusions for our models. |
| [Lee, Chen and Korbak: Training agents to self-report misbehavior](https://alignment.openai.com/self-incrimination/) | Opened original research post, March 21, 2026. Supports discussing trained self-report signals as a different intervention from prompted requirement accounting. |

The source abstracts/posts support the narrow descriptions used. This is not
a claim that all linked papers' code, data or full experimental protocols have
been reproduced. The suggested [MASK-OFF DOI](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/7OBJPU)
redirected to an inaccessible Harvard Dataverse page; it is omitted from the
substantive literature claims rather than assigned unverified findings.

## Submission requirements

The [official AI × Epistemics Research Sprint page](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/ai-x-epistemics-research-sprint-2026-11-13-to-2026-11-15)
was opened directly, including Guidelines and the submission form. It specifies:

- A PDF using the official template, at most eight main-text pages excluding references and appendices.
- A project title, a 150–250 word abstract, author names and affiliations.
- A Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations appendix.
- A report upload limit of 25 MB. Code, slides and a presentation recording are optional.
- Submission through the form on that page by November 15, 2026, 23:59 AoE (UTC−12): November 16, 11:59 UTC / 17:29 IST.

Its detailed layout therefore has **not** been verified. The draft has the
required content, and author metadata is user-confirmed. Template transfer,
actual PDF page count, visual inspection, upload and confirmation remain Sprint
11. A Markdown word count cannot certify the page limit. No submission has been
made, no PDF exported in Sprint 10, and no external terms accepted on the user's
behalf.

Do not substitute the repository's draft report template for a claim of using
the official template. Recheck the event page at submission time for changes.
